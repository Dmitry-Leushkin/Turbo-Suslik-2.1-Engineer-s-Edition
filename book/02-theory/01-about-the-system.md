# About the System

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

The techniques described in this book form the foundation of a system for independent work with the psyche. Many practitioners who work consistently and without self-deception notice changes in their state, perception, and reactions within a few months. There are no guarantees: the result depends on your initial condition, your honesty with yourself, and whether you actually process the material instead of looking for a magic solution.

All the techniques are based on direct work with the subconscious through previously installed programs. In this respect, Turbo-Suslik has something in common with BSFF, which you may have heard of or practiced with varying results. The important differences lie in the overall strategy, the depth of processing, and the degree of automation.

The technical mechanics of working with the subconscious will be covered in the next chapter. For now, it is enough to understand the basic operating model: you identify the material, formulate the target, and launch a protocol. The installed programs perform much of the routine processing without requiring you to consciously dissect every memory, reaction, and internal connection.

Strategy

The strategy of the system consists of four main areas.

1. Release from the Past

Current reactions are often powered by material accumulated in the past: traumatic episodes, resentment, fear, shame, conclusions made under pressure, and behavioral decisions that continue to run long after the original situation has ended. Turbo-Suslik processes this material together with the connections through which it still affects the present.

As the emotional charge decreases, the past gradually loses its ability to control current behavior. The facts and memories remain, but they stop automatically producing the same pain, anger, anxiety, or defensive reaction. This is not suppression, forgetting, or emotional numbing, and it does not make you colder or somehow less human. The material is not pushed deeper; it is processed until it no longer requires a forced reaction.

In practice, an episode that once knocked you out for hours or days may eventually become an ordinary archived record: you remember what happened, but there is nothing left to replay. The endpoint is complete indifference toward the processed material: it remains available as information but stops demanding a reaction.

This also reduces anxious “living in the future.” Much of the endless forecasting, threat anticipation, and compulsive planning is the past trying to prevent itself from happening again. When the source material stops being reactivated, less attention is dragged into imaginary futures, and it becomes easier to deal with what is actually happening now.

2. Depolarization of the Mind

A polarity is a mentally charged opposition: good–bad, must–must not, success–failure, acceptable–unacceptable, and so on. The problem is not the ability to distinguish one thing from another. The problem is the automatic charge that forces perception and behavior into one of two rigid positions.

The system consistently processes these polarities and the material attached to them. As the charge weakens, perception becomes less distorted by compulsory evaluation, avoidance, and desire. This gives you more room to see the situation as it is and act according to reality rather than according to an old internal command. Polarities and the practical methods for working with them will be covered later.

3. Clearing Mental Noise

Mental noise (later in the book as well called “garbage”) includes fear, guilt, shame, suppression, apathy, helplessness, anxiety, persistent worry, low self-esteem, lack of confidence, and limiting beliefs such as “I am a failure,” “people do not like me,” or “money is evil.” It also includes the thoughts, memories, bodily reactions, and repetitive internal dialogues that maintain these states.

It is not especially important whether this material “resides” in thought, emotion, memory, or the body. In reality, these elements are usually linked. What matters is that the active bundle can be identified, submitted for processing, and gradually removed from the mental circulation that consumes attention and energy every day.

Beliefs are not magically erased. However, once the emotional charge and accumulated evidence feeding a limiting belief are processed, it stops functioning as an unquestionable command. It becomes possible to see alternatives and act differently without having to overpower yourself through affirmations or forced positive thinking.

4. Integration of the Personality

The psyche is not a single, perfectly coordinated system. It is a collection of learned modes, habits, roles, defensive reactions, and behavioral programs that activate in different contexts. A person may function one way at work, another way at home, and a third way in close relationships. This is normal up to a point. The trouble begins when these modes contradict and sabotage one another.

One part wants change, another expects danger, a third preserves the familiar state, and a fourth invents a convincing explanation for why nothing can be done. The result is ambivalence, internal swings, self-sabotage, and the recurring impression that “I understand everything, but still do the opposite.”

Processing reduces the charge and rigid decisions that hold these conflicting modes in place. As the number and intensity of internal conflicts decrease, behavior becomes more coherent: less of the system is pulling in mutually exclusive directions.

Tactics

The tactical side is simpler.

1. Practice

Problems change through work with the material that produces and maintains them. Repeating safe affirmations for years, persuading yourself that everything is fine, or trying to “believe correctly” does not replace processing.

At times, you will have to go elbow-deep into your own mess. That is not the goal of the system; it is a temporary phase. The goal is to process the material, stop carrying it around, and return attention to living, working, creating, and dealing with reality. Digging without processing merely turns self-analysis into another hobby.

2. The Subconscious as a Working Tool

In this book, the word “subconscious” does not refer to a mystical entity or a wise inner being. It is a practical label for automatic processes of the psyche that already operate continuously outside direct conscious control.

Installed programs give these processes a defined task and processing logic. Your conscious role is to notice what is happening, turn a vague “I feel bad” into workable material- “I feel bad because…” - and launch the appropriate protocol. You do not need to consciously track every association and connection involved.

Awareness is still necessary. Automation cannot process a target that you persistently refuse to identify. At the same time, insights that are genuinely useful often surface on their own during the work, without being squeezed out through endless analysis.

3. Automation

The system uses a large number of installed programs to automate routine processing. Several processes can be launched on different layers of a problem and continue in the background, including during sleep.

Automation does not mean that one command removes an entire problem forever. Complex material usually has several connected layers and may need to be approached from different angles. The point is that a large volume of secondary material can be processed without manually walking through every episode and association. What would otherwise take years of repetitive conscious work becomes technically manageable.

Requirements

The system places several requirements on the practitioner.

1. Full Responsibility for Your Own Life

Without this, there is no point in starting. Responsibility does not mean that everything that happened to you was fair, deserved, or caused by you. It means that your decisions, reactions, choices, and further actions are now your responsibility, regardless of who created the original situation.

If control is permanently assigned outside - “they ruined my life,” “circumstances decide everything,” “nothing depends on me” - there is no working point from which change can begin. You remain an object in someone else’s causal chain.

During processing, recurring patterns often become visible: an early episode produced a belief or decision, that decision shaped later choices, and those choices repeatedly recreated similar situations in relationships, work, finances, or self-perception. This causal chain shows what can actually be processed and changed.

Meaningful work begins after responsibility is accepted. If you believe that someone else must change first, you can close the book. The system has nothing to work with.

2. Rejection of Mental Overanalysis

The task is not to construct the perfect explanation of why you feel the way you do. The task is to take the problem and process it.

Instead of searching indefinitely for external answers, you identify the components available now: the state, the trigger, the memories, the beliefs, the bodily reactions, and the internal objections. Then you submit them for processing. Thoughts such as “why,” “this cannot be solved,” “I already understand everything,” and “how could this possibly work?” are also material, not privileged commentary from an objective observer.

Analysis is useful when it helps you find the target. After that, it must give way to practical work. Otherwise, the mind can spend another ten years producing brilliant explanations for why the same problem is still there.

3. Willingness to Face What Arises

An honest look inside can reveal fear, hostility, envy, shame, helplessness, contradictory desires, and decisions that do not fit your preferred self-image. You do not need to admire or justify this material. You need to recognize it accurately enough to process it.

If entire areas are declared untouchable, the connections running through them remain active. The system can only work with what you are willing to put on the table.

4. Willingness to Process the Required Volume

The psyche is an interconnected network, not a folder containing a few isolated bad files. Deep patterns are supported by many episodes, reactions, beliefs, and compensatory behaviors. Changing them inevitably activates connected material.

The amount of work differs from person to person, but it is usually substantial. No honest method can promise deep restructuring in a weekend. You process as much material as your particular system contains - not as much as would be convenient. That is the actual price of the result.
