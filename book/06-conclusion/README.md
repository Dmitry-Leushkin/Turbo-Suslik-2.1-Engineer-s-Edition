# Conclusion

[Book contents](../README.md)

At this point, within the scope of this book, it's appropriate to put a full stop here. Over time - provided you don't quit mid-rollback and don't turn away from the pipeline - a fairly stable state, what could be called a **steady positive baseline**, is genuinely reachable. That's the actual target of all of this work. The hysteria, the drama, the strain, the need to constantly force yourself forward through heroic effort - all typical of the first few months - fades out. What's left is a calmer, simpler life, with a lot less unnecessary internal overhead running in the background.

Gradually, one thing becomes clearer, more and more often: the issue was never the hardware - it's the operator. The problem isn't some inherently broken or defective mind, and it isn't some "terrible ego" people love declaring war on. It's the missing skill of conscious self-management. TS makes that visible. It clears out the worn-down automatic patterns that used to spin endlessly on repeat, and because of that, life becomes noticeably different. Externally, though, it looks exactly the same as before - and that's the actual paradox in all of this change.

At later stages, you might get interested in other tools and approaches that pair well with TS. If so, the leveling and simplification process can move faster and land deeper. That's a natural continuation of the work, not a requirement anyone's imposing on you.

In the end, with TS you spend a lot less time in endless mental chewing, analysis, and internal struggle - and a lot more time just living. No imposed meaning, no manufactured drama, no constant internal tension running as a background process. Living, while letting other people live too. Noticing plain things - the air, sound, the seasons turning, the ordinary rhythm of a day. No rushing. Room to be imperfect. A sense of freshness in each new day. Moving through life at a steady pace, changing gradually - with no strain, and no wasted effort.
