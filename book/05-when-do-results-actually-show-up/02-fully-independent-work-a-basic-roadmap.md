# Fully Independent Work: A Basic Roadmap

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

Depending on whether you're running pre-built protocols or writing your own, the actual work plan can look different. Let's walk through the fully independent path specifically.

**Step one: process the past off a list.** At minimum: key episodes - humiliation, high-intensity emotional events, resentment, trauma, anything that left a real mark. Minor secondary items can wait; get the highest-charge material discharged first - same priority ordering as clearing P0s before P3s. Technically, this runs exactly as covered in the relevant chapter.

**Step two: compile a list of your own traits you dislike.** Character traits, habitual reactions, habits, behavior patterns. E.g.: `"intolerance toward alcoholics,"` `"sarcasm,"` `"phone dependency,"` `"need for attention,"` `"short fuse,"` `"procrastination,"` and so on. For each entry, you can write a dedicated protocol for automated processing, or fully describe every aspect of the item and feed the whole batch into the auto-processor. In practice, combining both approaches works best. Then move to the next item on the list, same procedure.

This gradually clears mental space. Over time, topics and problems surface that previously went unnoticed or didn't register as significant. Log those too, run them through the same pipeline. **Large belief clusters deserve dedicated, separate handling** - targeting them deliberately, not incidentally. Examples: `"all beliefs about money,"` `"all beliefs about what a man/woman should be,"` `"the meaning of life and everything attached to it."` Clusters like these typically contain hundreds, sometimes thousands, of individual sub-constructs - a lot of surface area, but still just mental material to clear. Any belief identified goes into processing, no exceptions. The reasoning for why that's safe, and doesn't erase functional skill, was already covered earlier.

As the work continues, it gets increasingly visible how beliefs and internal constructs actually shape subjective reality and drive downstream events. Usually a tangible, motivating realization on its own. That said - don't get stuck admiring the effect. Keep the pipeline running. Over time, resentment can surface - toward circumstances, toward "reality" itself, toward your own subconscious - because change feels too slow, or not shaped how it was expected to be. All of that - including any fixed idea of "how this should be going" - is itself just more material for the same pipeline. Disbelief and doubt need regular, dedicated attention specifically: as already covered, they're some of the most widespread, persistent sources of internal noise, and clearing them can take months on their own.

Pay particular attention to beliefs about your own capabilities and limits, plus any knowledge, theories, or fixed models about how "reality is structured." Fewer rigid conceptual structures loaded means higher adaptability and behavioral flexibility, full stop. Nothing to worry about here - practical skills like driving or using a computer don't get wiped. What gets cleared is excessive, rigid certainty about what's "possible" and what's "categorically impossible" - and losing that is almost always a net gain.

Material for processing surfaces constantly throughout this whole process. Don't let it slip past uncaptured. Always have a fast capture method on hand - phone notes, a voice recorder, whatever's frictionless. Keeps emerging thoughts, reactions, and states from getting lost before you can route them into processing later, calmly, on your own schedule.
