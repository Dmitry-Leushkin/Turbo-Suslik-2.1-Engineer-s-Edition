# How to Merge Polarities: Technical Reference

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

Below: the purely technical side of working with polarities. The procedure for neutralizing a polarity pair via the **Merge** processor is minimal.

Take any pair off the polarity list. Say, `Love - Hate`. Sequentially say - out loud or silently, no functional difference between the two - the token `Love`, then the token `Hate`, then the call `Merge`. Loop `Love - Hate - Merge` until a yawn fires, or up to ten iterations, whichever comes first. Then move to the next pair, or return to whatever else you were doing.

Early in working a specific pair, it can help to not run pure mechanical repetition, but to briefly **log the subjective content of each pole first**. In this example: notice what `Love` actually resolves to for you - what emotions, thoughts, or somatic markers get associated with the token. Do the same lookup for `Hate`. Then call `Merge`.

This isn't a required precondition. The neutralization runs even on a fully mechanical execution path with zero content lookup. But an initial read on what each pole actually contains usually speeds up convergence and improves the overall result. Some polarities - abstract or philosophical ones especially - resist a clean content lookup. That's fine, not an error state. The process runs regardless.

Some practitioners run `Merge` not to a yawn or fixed iteration count, but until a subjective sense of neutralization shows up. This variant can take anywhere from a few minutes to considerably longer, or produce no perceptible signal at all - depends on the individual. Possible outputs vary: sometimes a direct recognition that both poles were arbitrary mental constructs to begin with; sometimes a sense that the two were functionally the same object the whole time.

To be precise: these subjective effects are neither required nor diagnostic. They don't speed up actual integration and don't affect the end result. In practical terms they're just side output - logging noise, not the signal you're optimizing for. Whether or not you get a felt sense of neutralization mid-run, the process completes regardless - sometimes fast, sometimes with lag. What matters isn't the immediate readout, it's the cumulative result: measurable change in behavior and perception, not internal light shows.

For polarities shaped like `Me and [someone/something]` - e.g. `Me and my mother` - same procedure. `Me and my mother - Merge`, repeated to a yawn or up to ten cycles. More detail on running `Merge` against relationships with specific people or objects gets covered in later chapters.

There's also a simpler path: an automated polarity processor, available after registering on the site. Upload your polarity list, run the batch job, no manual involvement required. Strong recommendation, though: **don't run the automated version until at least two-thirds of the list has already been cleared manually.**

**Manual work in Phase 1 matters fundamentally.** It builds baseline sensitivity to the process and lays the groundwork everything after it depends on. Skip this stage, especially early, and you typically see reduced effectiveness in every phase downstream.
