# What Will You Get as a Result?

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

There's not much point stating anything definitive about the output you'll get after a month of Phase 1. People vary enormously: nervous-system baseline, volume and structure of accumulated backlog, adaptation speed, actual readiness for change. So the full range of outcomes is on the table - anywhere from "barely register a diff" to "this materially changed my whole state." Both are within normal operating parameters.

Which is why I won't describe how it "should" go, or what to expect. Any such spec easily turns into a source of manufactured expectation - and disappointment the moment observed behavior doesn't match the doc. This system deliberately avoids setting expectations as a config value, and it avoids benchmarking your run against someone else's output.

The only valid metric is your own direct observation of your own state, reactions, behavior. If something changes, you'll see the diff yourself. If change runs slow and quiet, that's also within spec. In recent years I genuinely can't recall a case where conscientious Phase 1 work produced zero measurable change - but that's an observation from the field, not a guarantee. There's no SLA here.

Whatever result you get is the correct result for your specific system. So instead of speculating about expected output ahead of time, the actual move is just running the job - if that's the decision you've made. But only start execution after reading the full book, not just Phase 1. I have zero interest in you entering this process without understanding the overall architecture and the range of possible side effects first.
