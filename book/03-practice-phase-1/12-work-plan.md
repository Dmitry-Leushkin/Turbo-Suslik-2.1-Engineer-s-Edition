# Work Plan

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

Over the next month - assuming you've made an actual decision to run this system rather than "take a quick look" - three baseline tasks need to ship:

1. Neutralize every polarity from the list covered in a previous chapter.
1. Process every emotional state on the emotional scale.
1. Launch **Cyber-Zhivo** and let it run autonomously for the full month.

Technical mechanics for all three are already covered above, no point repeating them. What matters here is different: **throughput.** How much material can you process daily and still get a clean result without overloading the system?

No universal answer exists. People vary enormously in sensitivity, current nervous-system state, and baseline internal tension. For one person, ten polarities a day is a light warm-up. For another, that's already a meaningful load. Any specific number here is a starting default, not a spec requirement.

For a sense of scale: early in my own work I ran about 20 polarities a day off the list, plus 20–30 emotional states. During the day I also fired `Process this` in parallel on whatever emotional material surfaced. Total daily throughput ended up fairly high. By evening there was usually a distinct fatigue - not physical, systemic: the same drained feeling you get after a genuinely demanding cognitive session, not a workout.

Conclusion is simple: overprocessing is a real failure mode, not a theoretical one.

So at the start, stick to a moderate default: **10–15 polarities and 10–15 emotional states per day.** After a few days, check the body's actual response carefully. Handling the load cleanly? Scale up gradually. Getting pronounced fatigue, irritability, apathy, or a general sense of being overloaded? That's a direct signal to throttle down.

Core principle: calibrate off bodily and emotional signal, not the mind's urge to "just push through and finish faster." The body, unlike the conscious mind, is usually an accurate rate-limiter - it tells you when load is sufficient, and it's rarely wrong about it. If it reads as "enough for today," it is enough. Sometimes a full rest day - zero processing - is the right call.

As the nervous system adapts, volume can scale up with no negative side effects. Even at later stages, though, the base rule holds: **never work through obvious exhaustion.** That's not a soft guideline, it's a hard constraint on the whole pipeline.

Within month one, you can also expand `Release` work by adding personal polarities specific to your own life. Neutralizing pairs like `me - [specific person]` often has a real positive effect on relationship quality and general perception of the world. That said, this is optional. At Phase 1, fully clearing the base polarity list is sufficient on its own.

Work on specific personal problems is best deferred to Phase 2, for two reasons. First: automated protocols make this work substantially easier and deeper, and they aren't available yet at this stage. Second: running intensive polarity work, emotion work, and personal-problem work concurrently can be excessive load for a system that hasn't adapted yet.

**Phase 1 is the foundation layer.** The task here is narrow and concrete: clear the polarities and the emotional clusters, at a sustainable pace, no heroics. Everything else can wait its turn in the queue.
