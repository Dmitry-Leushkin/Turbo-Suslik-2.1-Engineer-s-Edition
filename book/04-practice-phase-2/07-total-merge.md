# Total Merge

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

Applying the `Merge` protocol day-to-day deserves its own coverage. It's a minimal tool that, despite looking almost too simple to matter, produces genuinely measurable effects. Even after clearing the main lists, keep it in the toolkit and run it periodically.

As covered earlier in "Work Plan," `Merge` isn't scoped only to polarities pulled from a fixed list - it generalizes to anything in the psyche that reads as "out of alignment." Logical starting point: people you actually interact with. First target - the "inner circle": spouse, parents, children, siblings, sometimes grandparents. Running `Merge` here doesn't auto-resolve every relationship issue on its own - specific irritating friction points still need a dedicated `Process this` pass. But the general subjective quality of contact, and the background tone of the interaction, usually shifts noticeably.

`Merge` also applies to deceased loved ones. Not a metaphysical claim here - it's processing your internal representation and any unfinished linkage still held in memory. Sometimes this surfaces unexpected reinterpretations tied to loss and the relationship itself.

Procedure's minimal. A formula like `"Me and my mother - Merge"`, aloud or silently, looped to a yawn, or up to 10 repetitions if no physiological signal fires. That's the entire call.

Past the inner circle, move outward: second- and third-circle relatives, friends, then acquaintances, neighbors, coworkers, managers. Same logic throughout - you're never operating on the real external person, only on how they're instantiated in your own perception and memory.

From there, scope expands basically without limit. Good place to start: anyone or anything currently generating irritation or tension. Sometimes it helps to chain `Merge` with other tools - `Process this` and `Hoppo`, for instance. Say a specific social group triggers you. First, capture every thought, judgment, and feeling that surfaces - written down, until the stream runs dry. Then process each entry individually via `Process this`. Then run `Hoppo` on the group to strip residual emotional charge. Finally, `Merge` against that object, person, or group.

Same pattern applies to any trigger category: age groups, neighbors, kids, different behavioral styles, authority figures, professions, whatever. Over time, the total count of active external triggers typically drops. Doesn't mean other people's actions automatically stop being objectively unpleasant - that still needs targeted work on specific situations and reactions. But the ambient background irritation level, at baseline, comes down. Sometimes even the subjective encounter rate shifts: attention stops latching onto these situations, and they simply surface less often in active experience.

`Merge` also applies to whatever attention happens to be pointed at in the moment: natural objects, strangers on the street, ambient sound, environmental elements. Here the effect isn't a change in the external world - it's a change in the observer's own state: reduced separation, less tension, a stronger sense of involvement. Subjectively this often reads as pleasant and stabilizing.

Formally, the target object of `Merge` can be almost anything: skills, activity types, personality traits, emotional states, bodily sensations. You can work an unwanted state or symptom directly by first isolating it as precisely as possible - intensity, location, dynamics - then running the standard formula: `"Me and [X] - Merge"`. Can produce a noticeable subjective shift. To get better at isolating states cleanly, learn the basics of classical aspecting: how to break a state into parts and process them separately.

Some people run `Merge` in a purely utilitarian mode - as a quick way to cut internal resistance or tension right before action. Waiting, uncertainty, negotiations, difficult interactions. Important to be precise: this isn't about controlling external reality. It's about shifting your own state, attention, and behavior - which can indirectly influence how events actually unfold, as a downstream effect, not a direct lever.

`Merge` can run in delayed mode (to yawn or a fixed rep count) or in real time - held until a subjective sense of reduced separation or tension shows up directly. This mode can produce vivid experiences and insight, especially early in practice. That said, the system overall is oriented around delayed, high-volume batch processing. Treat real-time `Merge` as a supplementary tool, not a goal in itself or an excuse to chase experiences for their own sake.

Bottom line: `Merge` **is a flexible, low-overhead utility for working with perception and internal boundaries.** It's not built for deep processing of complex material - but it pairs well with the other protocols. Its effects trace to changes in how perception and attention are organized, not to anything mystical. Within that scope, it's genuinely useful. Keep it in active rotation.
