# Beliefs

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

After clearing a large layer of material, one thing became obvious: charge from the past isn't the only mechanism holding problems in place - often it isn't even the main one. Emotionally loaded episodes matter, and you do need to run them through the pipeline. But more often, the actual structure holding a problem up is built from beliefs, conclusions, "known facts," decisions, and internal rules - accepted as ground truth at some point and never revisited. These are mental constructs: they exist purely in the mind, but they define the perception filter, the available choices, the reaction set, and, downstream of all that, the trajectory of an entire life.

The most damaging are **limiting beliefs** - hardcoded assertions where you're "absolutely certain" something is impossible, unrealistic, "just doesn't happen," "nonsense." Run processing on these, and it becomes obvious how much of this junk is buried in the config, silently returning `false` for entire categories of input, quietly killing initiative, narrowing available life-space far below what's actually possible.

My approach: **total processing.** Everything you consider "true" about yourself, the world, other people goes into the pipeline: beliefs, conclusions, interpretations, assumptions, internal prohibitions, "this is how it should be," "this isn't allowed." No values in this config get exempted from review. Even the ones dressed up as "laws of life" are still your own internal models - probabilistic priors your brain uses to predict reality and drive behavior. As long as you treat a prior as objective ground truth instead of a prior, it controls you - through attention, decisions, automatic reactions. I'll get to what remains useful below. First, the rule: everything gets processed, no exceptions, no privileged config.

At some point you start seeing something uncomfortable: a huge share of what you believed originated randomly - a couple of episodes, some phrases, shame, self-protection, or just a need to explain what was happening at the time. And then this randomly-generated content went on to drive your choices and your life for years. At this stage people frequently land on "reality is illusory." Which is itself just another thought - meaning it's also material, and gets fed straight back into the same pipeline.

The goal isn't swapping one philosophy for another. The goal is stepping out of being captured by concepts at all - perceiving what's happening without compulsively tagging everything `true/false`, `real/unreal`. At that point concepts become a tool instead of a cage: a swappable strategy, not a hardcoded implementation. You can still consciously pick a worldview, fully aware of its conditional nature, without it silently running you. Once you're there, clever talk about "the nature of reality" stops having any hypnotic pull - you can see it's a model, not a revelation. Models can be useful or harmful. They were never required to be "true" to begin with.

So what remains useful? Sound logic doesn't break under processing. In this sense TS runs as a **decomposer**: you discharge the emotional and semantic bindings, split the construct into its elements, and strip the automatic tension off it. Anything genuinely functional tends not to be built on compulsion or fear - it's built on real reasons, real skill, actual tested experience. So post-discharge, it reconverges fast, sometimes without you even noticing the gap. What's dysfunctional, compulsive, harmful, on the other hand, is usually load-bearing on nothing but charge, shame, anxiety, and unexamined conclusions - spaghetti held together entirely by hidden global state. Strip the charge, untangle the coupling, and it loses its pull and falls out of the runtime on its own.

Post-processing, you don't become incapable of thinking the old way. You just stop being forced into it as the default execution path. Any psychological block is a knot of unawareness, held together by discomfort and automatic defensive routines. Untangle the layered knot and you get clean cause-and-effect visibility - full observability into what triggers what, how a given conclusion actually forms, why a habit persists.

At that point the same belief, if you genuinely want it, can be rebuilt - deliberately, step by step, with the cost fully priced in. Want to go back to running `everyone_suffers_at_work = true`*?* You can. But now it's an explicit override you opted into consciously, not an implicit default silently running your life - and you know exactly what you're trading away by choosing it. This is exactly why anything actually healthy tends to survive processing intact: it was never built on nervous static and bad logic to begin with. And if something keeps pulling you back toward familiar junk - you already have the fix. Write it down, decompose it, fire the processor, move on. Repeat as many times as the queue requires.

Try inspecting the base-layer beliefs most people never even register as beliefs - they read as plain facts. Do you deserve happiness? Ordinary joy? Work that's actually meaningful and satisfying? Prosperity? Calm relationships? However it sounds, internal beliefs really do compile down into behavioral patterns, and behavior compiles down into recurring real-world events - this is the one true thing buried inside a lot of badly-explained "manifestation" content; the mechanism's real, the explanation's just garbage. Set these defaults consciously. Almost nobody ever gets shown how - not in childhood, not later.

Over time, a specific state can emerge: you hold a model without being captured by it. You believe it, without self-deceiving about what it is. You run a chosen worldview as a working hypothesis, fully aware it's conditional - which produces genuine lightness and calm. Sometimes there's a pull to test different lenses, just to see how perception, and downstream of it behavior and events, shifts under a different set of priors. That fluidity doesn't come from talk or philosophy, though. It comes from practice: processing your beliefs and internal rules, repeatedly, over and over.

Principle stays simple: process every belief. No exceptions.
