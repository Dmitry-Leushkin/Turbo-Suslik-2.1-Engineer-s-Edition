# Circular Processing

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

In its pure form, circular processing isn't used in this system. More precisely: its logic is already embedded inside the processors and automated protocols - I don't see a reason to run it manually when it's already handled at the runtime level. Since the term's already surfaced earlier, though, here's a quick technical breakdown of what it means, what actually runs under it, and why it's "circular."

**Circular processing** means working not just your own position, but also **the internal models of other people or entities linked to the material being processed**. Goal: leave no unhandled objects in memory that could keep the problem alive from an unreferenced pointer. During a manual run, attention sequentially shifts across the different "viewpoints" tied to the situation and processes whatever material surfaces at each one.

Sounds strange at first - how do you "process something on behalf of someone else"? In practice, though, you're never actually operating on the real external person. You're operating on their **local instance** - the internal model your own mind constructed of them. Every interaction you've ever had doesn't run against the actual person directly; it runs against your local copy of them: a bundle of perceptions, expectations, interpretations, emotions, cached and stored on your end. So when you're processing a problem, images of the people involved almost always surface, and inside your internal picture, each one carries its own "stance" toward what's happening and toward your attempts to change it.

Concrete case: problem being processed is `"I hate my neighbor."` You process the material from your own side, then check: does any object in my internal model register as opposed to this change? Answer's usually obvious - the neighbor's local instance surfaces immediately. In classical circular processing, next step is switching identification to that instance and running the material attached to it. Important distinction: this is never "processing the actual neighbor." It's processing how the neighbor is instantiated inside your own psyche.

Holding attention on that local model, accumulated material starts surfacing: judgments, accusations, irritation, imagery, grievances - everything that was previously running quietly in the background, unflagged. That material gets logged and processed the normal way. Once discharged, attention returns to the original context and the loop closes.

The logic here is straightforward: even if your own side reads as fully processed, other participants' local instances can still be sitting in memory holding a reference to the old state, keeping tension and the old scenario alive from a spot you never checked. Circular processing exists specifically to clear those residual references too. Which is why plenty of authors working with adjacent techniques recommend always accounting for these "internal oppositions," however odd they look on the surface.

In practice, people sometimes find the most unexpected objects "objecting" inside their own model: relatives, abstract authority figures, animals, symbolic imagery. From a pure processing standpoint, that's irrelevant. If there's a charge attached to an object in your internal state, discharge it - whether the object itself is logical or absurd. You're never operating on external reality here. You're operating on internal state.

This system doesn't run classical manual circular processing - full stop. It's heavy, exhausting, and doesn't outperform the automated version. The logic is already baked into the processors and protocols - including the ones you're already running constantly - so material gets covered thoroughly without you having to manually enumerate every possible object holding a reference to the problem. Same principle as garbage collection handling reachability automatically instead of requiring the programmer to trace every pointer by hand.

So when running protocols, there's no separate circular-processing step required - it's already part of the mechanism. This chapter exists purely to explain what's actually running under the hood, and why the system holds up reliably without manually walking every role in the scenario.
