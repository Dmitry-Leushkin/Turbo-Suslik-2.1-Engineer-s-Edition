# Processing the Past

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

Working with the past in Phase 2 is one of the priority tracks at this stage - priority at least until the primary emotional charge tied to past events has been substantially reduced.

This isn't about erasing the past or "forgetting" it. Events don't get deleted - they gradually lose the ability to auto-trigger an emotional reaction. In practice: the past starts reading as neutral archival information - "it happened, logged, done" - with no standing tension, no replay loop, no residual charge on read.

With regular, consistent work, that state is largely reachable in about two months, and by three to four months the past typically stops materially affecting your current runtime. Timeline's individual, obviously - depends on work intensity, tooling used, and overall nervous-system baseline.

The past gets touched, in some form, by nearly every automated protocol regardless. Even so, at this stage, start deliberately with the most significant episodes and key people first - highest-severity tickets, essentially. Some of this runs manually, some via automation. Either way, the entry point is the same: **compile the list.**

Compiling Lists

Work on the past starts with logging charged episodes from your life.

Pick any medium that works - phone notes, a file on your computer, a physical notebook - and start writing down events as they surface. No analysis, no scoring "how important is this," no attempt to build a priority hierarchy up front. Just log whatever comes up.

At the start, only a handful of episodes may surface - that's expected. As you keep working, memory access gradually warms up, like a cold cache filling in: more of the past becomes reachable over time. A lot of records are inaccessible early precisely because of how painful they are - flagged, essentially, with restricted read access - but they become retrievable later. Don't force a full dump on the first pass. Pull what surfaces naturally.

Write short - tags, not full narratives. E.g.:

- `"father hit me on my birthday, 7th grade"`
- `"mother slapped me in front of everyone at a meeting"`
- `"kids took my money when I was 8"`
- `"embarrassing wardrobe incident during PE, second year"`

The subconscious doesn't need a full writeup. All the detail is already stored on its end - you only need a **tag** per episode, a short identifier you'll use later to invoke processing on it.

Where possible, group episodes by person. If several events tie to one individual, log them together - same reasoning as grouping tickets by component. This matters because after the individual episodes get processed, there's a separate pass targeting that person's overall image specifically, to strip whatever residual charge is still attached to them as an entity, not just to the individual incidents.

Some Context

Anyone familiar with "recapitulation" from other past-work traditions will recognize the list-building approach here. TS runs similar logic - minus the ritual, minus "retrieving energy," minus any metaphysics layered on top. We're not re-living the past. We're tagging the material and stripping the charge off it.

What Exactly to Log

Your job: log as many charged episodes as you can find, prioritizing:

- anything involving parents;
- anything painful;
- anything that produces discomfort on recall.

"Charge" here means any residual "not okay" signal on read: tension, shame, anger, sadness, irritation. If an episode replays with zero reaction, its charge is probably already near-zero - low priority, not worth chasing.

**Important:** no need to re-live episodes, sit in the feeling, or deliberately try to "feel" anything. Log the fact, set it aside for the actual processing pass later.

On average, practitioners log 100 to 300 episodes. The real total's higher - minor, low-severity ones often don't surface on manual recall, which is fine; those get swept up later by automation anyway.

Best to start with the heaviest, most painful entries - clear P0s first. Processing those produces noticeable relief fast and meaningfully boosts motivation to keep going. Over time the list starts filling with progressively smaller items - `"a bus conductor was rude,"` `"didn't get my change back"` - down to the point where it's obvious the backlog's basically drained. That's a normal, expected signal that manual work on the past is done and can hand off entirely to automation.

Sometimes, months later, deep foundational incidents - usually from childhood - surface that weren't accessible before. Read access on those records finally cleared. When that happens, go back and run a targeted manual pass on them specifically.

Episodes can get processed as they surface or pulled from the list later - no single correct method here, pick whichever workflow fits you better.

If Nothing Comes to Mind

Sometimes people hit a state of "genuinely nothing surfaces." Can cause confusion, and a false read of "there's nothing here to process."

Theoretically, this is possible as a result of already-achieved acceptance and internal integration. But in that case life is usually already running fine, with no standing background tension. If that's actually your situation - reasonable question: why are you even running this system? Go live your life.

In practice, though, the cause is usually the opposite. No memories surfacing, and life is nowhere close to calm and clear? That means the past was heavy enough that the psyche straight-up denied read access to it. Suppression and sealing off memory is a standard protective mechanism - access control working as designed, not a bug.

For this group, the work runs harder, especially early on, and rollback swings tend to run stronger. The standard-pace approach may not be the right config here. In that case, throttle immediately down to minimum: run automations only, neutralize 2–3 polarities a day max, and only touch emotion work when there's a clear signal from the system that it's ready. Forcing throughput here gets met with heavy internal resistance and multiplies the total timeline. This isn't "it's broken" - it's the system flagging that the load you assigned exceeds what your current state can actually handle.

Usually, past the initial freeze, the process does start, and the list starts filling in fairly easily from there.
