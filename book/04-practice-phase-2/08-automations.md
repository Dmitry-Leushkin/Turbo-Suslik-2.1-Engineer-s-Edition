# Automations

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

The strongest part of this whole system is the ability to almost fully automate processing of large volumes of psychological material and push the work into the background. There's no upside I can see in spending significant manual time digging through a pile of internal junk by hand. Far more rational to hit a button, hand the job to subconscious mechanisms, and go on living your actual life while it runs.

Sometimes there's pushback: only bringing material into full conscious awareness "guarantees" it actually gets processed and disappears. That's a stance, a habitual working model - not some universal constraint on the system. If someone's genuinely interested in spending years manually dissecting every internal construct one by one, savoring the awareness process itself - that's a valid choice, just not mine. My target is straightforward: clear the backlog as fast as possible, stop the generator that keeps refilling it, ship, move on.

To be precise: the goal isn't just clearing a fixed backlog of accumulated material. It's changing the actual **generator function** producing it continuously. Most of this "junk" exists purely in subjective psychological state and has no direct mapping to objective reality. Most people run their own mental hardware in a way that keeps reproducing the same tension, the same reactions, the same interpretations - feeding a constant stream of internal noise. Clear that material and you get two effects: attention gets freed up, and you start seeing more clearly which internal moves actually generate problems and which don't. Anyone interested can even track this objectively - EEG comparisons before starting versus a few months in. Not a required part of the work, but interesting as a data point if you're curious.

For me personally, the core value of the whole system is the automation itself. Not the requirement for constant analysis - the fact that you can define an algorithm once and let it run unattended. If someone had described this approach to me a few years before I built it, I'd have been skeptical it would actually hold up. Practice showed it's not just possible - it's effective, even if convergence isn't always linear. The method's minor limitations don't come close to outweighing the main advantage: processing large volumes of material with zero ongoing conscious involvement required. Automated protocols turned out to be the closest thing to an actual "magic pill" that exists - if by that you mean stable change within a reasonable timeframe, not an instant effect.

At the core, every automation - what we call a **protocol** - is a detailed instruction for subconscious execution. It specifies material type, processing method, and expected timeframe. Read the instruction once, then the user just needs to fire it via the defined trigger phrase and get back to whatever else they were doing.

You get introduced to the first one of these fairly early: **Cyber-Zhivo**, which runs a baseline clearing pass on the past against a predefined algorithm. Once you clear the initial phase - polarities and emotional clusters - you move on to other entry-level protocols scoped to beliefs, fears, and a range of states. As practice progresses, their effectiveness becomes obvious enough to justify continuing. Two paths from there: write your own protocols, which needs some understanding of the underlying principles but is broadly accessible, or use existing, more specialized ones already built. In practice, most people eventually end up writing their own regardless - impossible to pre-anticipate every individual state in advance.

Most protocols, as a side effect of their own execution, also touch past material - so even without targeted work on the past specifically, its subjective load drops over time as a byproduct. Where needed, you can build protocols for specific states, beliefs, situations, or even for interaction with particular people, in the sense of how they're represented in your own perception.

Over time, as experience accumulates, you can build a protocol for basically any task. Theoretically, you could even reproduce the effects of various "spiritual" or esoteric practices as functional emulations, triggered by a single phrase. Not something I'd recommend running before your overall state's stabilized and the base clearing is done, though. Also factor this in: a side effect of deep processing is that a lot of previous beliefs stop being persuasive. Don't be surprised if something that once felt profound or inspiring starts reading as ironic instead. At some point interest in this kind of experiment can drop off entirely - or it can come back, but now as conscious play with models rather than actual belief in them.

Using protocols is minimal. Read the protocol once, aloud or silently - doesn't matter. Then fire it by repeating the trigger phrase until a yawn or comparable signal fires. One invocation. Re-running the same protocol multiple times doesn't do anything useful - it just adds unnecessary load and can trigger fatigue or discomfort. The whole operating logic: **read it, run it, get back to your life**. Everything downstream executes in the background without further involvement required.
